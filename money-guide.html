Warning: you lose your money at the end of Chapter 1

This is the biggest money mistake players are making in launch week. Game Rant, PowerPyx and a widely shared r/GTA6 warning (reported by ComicBook) all say the same thing: when Chapter 1 ends, Jason and Lucia lose their pocket cash, their BuckMe current account balance and the stash in Jason’s house. Game Rant adds that you also lose Jason’s house and car. The point of no return is the mission Tourist Trap, which locks you into three back-to-back missions (Game Rant).

What carries over, according to Game Rant: money in BuckMe Family Savings (up to the $5,000 Chapter 1 limit), clothes you bought, and guns, which you can take back from the weapons locker at Ammu-Nation in Chapter 2. So before Tourist Trap:

  1. Empty the stash in Jason’s house.
  2. Move $5,000 into Family Savings in the BuckMe app.
  3. Spend what is left on guns, attachments or clothes. Don’t spend it on upgrading Jason’s car.
  4. Finish the Chapter 1 side activities you care about. GamesRadar+ says Kenton’s shipwrecks and Cal’s fishing are only temporarily disabled, not lost.

How the GTA 6 economy works: cash, BuckMe and Family Savings

  • Cash on hand is what you carry. It can be lost if you go Wasted or Busted, so deposit at an ATM after every big job. PC Gamer still suggests keeping a few hundred dollars in your pocket.
  • BuckMe current (checking) account is your bank balance. Jason and Lucia each have their own (gta.guide, Dork); only Family Savings is shared. Its limit starts at $15,000 and rises with the story to $2,000,000; payments wait as pending when it is full (Game Rant). It is safer than cash, but Game Rant says the police take a bail fee from it when you are arrested. Some buyers, such as Pay ’n’ Spray, pay straight into BuckMe.
  • BuckMe Family Savings can never be withdrawn and can never be lost (Game Rant). The Chapter 1 limit is $5,000 and it rises as the story goes on; PowerPyx says the final Save for the Future goal is $2 million. Only put in money you will not need for story targets. All six goals, perks and how much to save per chapter: GTA 6 Family Savings and BuckMe explained.
  • Loot (jewelry, drugs, marked bills, stolen cars) is not money until a fence or buyer takes it. GamesRadar+ notes that fences refuse marked bills until you have the Dye-Pack Jammer from the Chapter 3 mission Credit Check.

Rockstar has not published a list of player-owned businesses or passive property income for story mode, and we will not invent one.

Selling cars: Moses, Pay ’n’ Spray and Randy

Cars are the steadiest money in the first half of the game. Full walkthrough: how to sell cars in GTA 6 (every buyer, limits, stored cars, Advanced Trackers). Scan every car with WAiNK first (hold Up on the D-pad, aim, press R1/RB) to see its value, tracker and which buyer wants it. Then match the car to the buyer:

  • No tracker: Moses in Port Gellhorn from Chapter 2. How to sell cars to Moses.
  • Standard Tracker: any Pay ’n’ Spray from early Chapter 3. Pay ’n’ Spray rules and limits.
  • Advanced Tracker supercars: Randy’s Wish List from mid Chapter 3; any Pay ’n’ Spray only from Chapter 4, after Randy’s ’64 Mamba GT job (Game Rant, Destructoid). The last car on his list is the Truffade Thrax, the fastest car in GTA 6 by stock Speed rating.
  • GTA+ members get 10% more when selling owned vehicles, according to Rockstar’s GTA+ page (as reported by Polygon). Details in our GTA+ section.
  • Edition cars (the Ultimate Edition’s ’95 Grotti Cheetah, the Vintage pack’s ’55 Vapid Stanier) are collectibles, not money makers. See Standard vs Ultimate Edition and the Vintage Vice City Pack.

Money FAQs

What is the fastest way to make money in GTA 6?

It depends on the chapter. PowerPyx and PC Gamer agree: in Chapter 1 just play the story; in Chapter 2 sell untracked cars to Moses (about $1,000+ each); in Chapter 3 rob jewelry stores ($10,000–$25,000), sell Standard Tracker cars at Pay ’n’ Spray and deliver Randy’s Wish List cars ($3,150–$20,200 base each plus a clean-condition bonus); from the end of Chapter 4, large bank vaults pay up to about $205,000. Payouts are launch-week reports, not official Rockstar figures. We do not cover cheats or glitches.

Do you lose your money at the end of Chapter 1 in GTA 6?

Yes. Game Rant and PowerPyx report that when Chapter 1 ends (the point of no return is the mission Tourist Trap) you lose your pocket cash, your BuckMe current account balance and the stash in Jason’s house. Only money in BuckMe Family Savings is kept. Put up to $5,000 into Family Savings first and spend the rest on guns or clothes, which carry over. Full checklist in the early-game guide.

Can you withdraw money from Family Savings in GTA 6?

No. Game Rant reports that money moved into BuckMe Family Savings can never be withdrawn, but it can never be lost either (not on death, arrest or the Chapter 1 reset). In Chapter 1 you can only deposit $5,000; the limit rises later. Do not lock away money you need for story goals such as the $15,000 Prepare for Vice City target.

Do you lose money when you die in GTA 6?

Cash you are carrying and loot bags can be lost if you go Wasted or Busted, so deposit at ATMs after every big job. Outlets disagree on how much of your BuckMe current account a death or arrest costs (Game Rant says arrests take a bail fee from it). Family Savings is never touched.

How much money do you need for Vice City in GTA 6?

PowerPyx’s walkthrough says the late Chapter 2 mission Prepare for Vice City asks Jason and Lucia to earn at least $15,000 while Dre’Quan finds them a place. Reaching it (and clearing the other Chapter 2 missions) unlocks Checking Out, the move to Vice City. Fastest route: our Chapter 2 plan.

Can you buy businesses in GTA 6?

Rockstar has not published a list of player-owned businesses or passive income properties for GTA 6 story mode, and the launch-week money guides we checked (PowerPyx, PC Gamer, GameSpot) do not list any. Treat claims of GTA Online-style businesses with caution until Rockstar confirms them.
