GTA 6 late-game money guide: snap gun robberies, Randy, and high-payout jobs

Once the story reaches Vice City, the snap gun, Randy’s high-end cars, and later Pilot Pete drops become the honest ways to scale your bank balance — without touching cheats or vault sieges you do not have time for.

Published 19 November 2026 · Updated 23 November 2026. Spoiler warning: tools and activities that unlock in Chapters 3 and 4. No ending details.

Updated 23 November 2026. New: how to rob clean and keep heat low (witness and alarm rules, CCTV, heat icons). On 22 November we added jewelry store robberies for Yasmani ($10,000–$25,000, PowerPyx), the Credit Check / Dye-Pack Jammer unlock for banks and Payday Loans (GamesRadar+), Randy’s 18-car Wish List and payouts, Standard Tracker sales at Pay ’n’ Spray, and the end-of-Chapter 4 bank vaults (up to about $205,000). Everything below is intended in-game crime — systems built into free roam — not cheats, glitches, or trainers. Payouts are launch-week reports from PowerPyx, Polygon, PC Gamer and GameSpot. The snap gun changes everything

PC Gamer and Polygon both flag the snap gun, unlocked as part of the Chapter 3 story, as the turning point. It lets you pick locks and bypass alarms, which means a back door is suddenly better than a front-door stick-up.

Back-door store and jewelry jobs

The method described independently by both outlets:

  1. Zoom the map (Polygon suggests 2D) and look for a store with a side or back alley.
  2. Enter through the back with the snap gun so the front of house does not instantly panic.
  3. Quietly deal with anyone in the rear rooms.
  4. Disable the alarm and wipe the security footage.
  5. Find the safe code on a monitor or a handwritten note, or get it from staff, instead of wandering into the sales floor.
  6. Empty the safe and, if you have time and a partner on crowd control, the cases and registers.
  7. Leave the way you came. If someone has already called the police, grab the take and get out — do not tour the main store.

Reported payouts: PC Gamer puts clean back-door jobs at about $6,000–$15,000. Polygon says many jewelry stores are worth roughly $20,000 and names Victoria Lee Jeweler in Capri and Blanc and Giroux in the Decor District as strong examples. If you have not unlocked the snap gun yet, Polygon says you can still kick a door and rush the alarm panel, but the clean, no-star version is a Chapter 3 skill.

These are designed heists, not map glitches. Take what the safe and cases give you, leave, and bank the cash before the next job.

Jewelry stores for Yasmani (early Chapter 3)

PowerPyx calls jewelry stores the big early-Chapter 3 earner. They are introduced by the main mission Yasmani’s Jewelry Jobs, and each robbery pays about $10,000–$25,000. What to know:

  • Yasmani, the fence you meet in the Chapter 3 story, pays a high price for stolen jewelry, so sell it to him.
  • Open the safe in the back room as well as the display cases; the back-door snap-gun method above makes this much easier.
  • You can rob several stores in a row. If you are quick, you can leave before the police arrive.
  • Polygon names Victoria Lee Jeweler in Capri and Blanc and Giroux in the Decor District as strong targets.

Bank the take at an ATM before the next job: cash on hand can be lost if you go Wasted.

Banks and Payday Loans (after Credit Check)

Bank cash comes as marked bills with dye packs. GamesRadar+ reports that fences will not accept marked bills, and the dye pack will cover you in pink paint, until you complete Credit Check, the 16th mission of Chapter 3, where Karim gives you the Dye-Pack Jammer. It is part of the main story and cannot be missed. After that you can rob small banks and Payday Loan stores and sell the bills. PowerPyx adds that robbing two Payday Loans is one of Yasmani’s jobs, and finishing it is needed later for bank vault tips.

Quick bank stick-ups (when you want speed, not the vault)

Polygon and GameSpot both warn that full bank vault jobs are profitable and also slow, loud, and easy to botch. For fast money, Polygon’s launch advice is more modest: once bank robberies unlock near the end of Chapter 3, walk into a large branch (they mention Lombank on Ocean Beach and downtown), put a gun on a guard immediately, have your partner control the room, empty the registers, and leave when sirens start. They estimate about $15,000 a visit if you do not get greedy.

GameSpot adds that partnering with professional bank robbers later in the story, and tools such as dye-pack jammers, improve serious bank work. That is a different guide — a planned heist, not a five-minute loop.

Randy and high-end cars

If Moses in Port Gellhorn was your Chapter 2 fence, Randy is the Vice City upgrade. GameSpot places him in western Vice City. He is after flashier, higher-end cars, pays more, and — importantly — launch coverage says he gives you a clone key (Polygon: near the end of Chapter 3) so you can get into modern cars that a slim jim will not touch. He also hands out a list of cars to steal (his Wish List, which ends with the Truffade Thrax, the fastest car in GTA 6 so far). Condition matters: a clean delivery pays more than a smashed one.

Randy’s Wish List, verified details (PowerPyx): halfway through Chapter 3 the main mission Clonin’ Keys, Makin’ GS (steal the Zorrusso for Randy) gives you the Key Cloner and unlocks the Wish List of 18 high-end sports cars. Deliveries usually pay $30,000+ if the car is in perfect condition. You can find the cars driving around Vice City, Randy marks a few parked ones, and you can text him from the phone’s Reminders section for more location tips. Push Square notes Wish List cars carry trackers, so keep your speed up so police cannot trace you; see the vehicles hub for tracker rules.

PC Gamer’s overall stolen-car range, depending on the buyer and the car, is $1,000–$20,000. Randy sits at the high end of that spread; Moses remains useful for quieter, older cars.

Pay ’n’ Spray, especially on Ocean Beach

PowerPyx reports that early in Chapter 3 you can sell stolen cars at any Pay ’n’ Spray, including cars with a Standard Tracker, which pay more (around $4,000+ for a good car). Launch guides report a limit of three Pay ’n’ Spray sales per in-game day.

You still do not need a named fence for every sale. Polygon’s fastest car loop is: steal an untracked (or at least not advanced-tracker) car next to a Pay ’N’ Spray, preferably from a parking lot rather than a moving driver, and sell it immediately. They highlight the shop on Ocean Beach and say a valuable nearby car can bring about $7,000 in roughly half a minute, with a short cooldown between sales. Cash out at an ATM between loops so you are not carrying a stack when you die.

Check the vehicles guide for officially named edition cars — those are collectible toys, not necessarily the best things to sell.

High-payout Chapter 4 work: Pilot Pete

PC Gamer says you meet a pilot in Chapter 3 and he offers regular work in Chapter 4. Pilot Pete sends you up in his plane to drop packages to waiting buyers around Leonida on a timer. Plan the route, do not miss drop windows, and the reported take is $10,000–$20,000 per run. This is one of the better late repeatable jobs if you are comfortable flying. Practice before you take a timed contract seriously.

Bank vaults: the biggest payout (end of Chapter 4)

Spoiler-light. PowerPyx calls large bank vaults “the absolute fastest way” to make money: up to about $205,000 for roughly five minutes of work. They unlock at the very end of Chapter 4, before the ending missions, through the optional mission That’s Where The Money Is… (Wait at the Cafe), which teaches the method:

  1. Follow a security van until it stops at a large bank and the guards open the vault to carry money in.
  2. Follow the guard and get into the vault while it is open.
  3. Expect an instant 4-star wanted level. PowerPyx suggests a fast motorcycle to cut through traffic, then switching vehicles so you are harder to recognize.
  4. After the tutorial, Yasmani sells security-van tips for $10,000, once you have finished his earlier jobs (including robbing two Payday Loans).

PowerPyx also notes the story pays you $120,000 automatically at the end of Chapter 3, and that the final Family Savings goal (Save for the Future) is $2 million, which is what most players will be saving vault money toward.

Full walkthrough: all 8 vault locations with payouts, the 14 small banks, Yasmani’s van tips and the escape plan are in our GTA 6 bank vaults guide.

A legal-ish alternative: Ambrosia Raceway demolition derby

Not every late-game dollar has to come from a felony. PC Gamer recommends the demolition races at Ambrosia Raceway — useful if you are a messy driver. You can rent a track car for a few hundred dollars. Wins are reported at about $3,800 on the oval and roughly double that (around $6,300) on the figure-eight, and the races are repeatable. The “secret,” in that write-up, is that destroying the field counts; you do not have to be a clean-lap hero. Ambrosia is one of Leonida’s six official regions, so you will already be passing through for the story or the map guide.

Methods that still work — and a few that usually do not

Still excellent: robbing other criminal crews mid-job ($1,000–$15,000 in PC Gamer’s range), especially with both characters so you can carry every bag; hunting for Bret when you are already in the north (see the Chapter 2 guide); and gang hideouts for a large but violent payday (GameSpot).

Usually not “fast”: mugging (a couple of hundred dollars); Kenton’s dives (still peaceful, now too slow compared with snap-gun jobs); yanking towable ATMs (PC Gamer: loud, slow, and great at summoning police); and full vault assaults when you only needed walking-around money.

We will not publish money cheats. If you see a post-story cash cheat advertised elsewhere, remember that unofficial reports already say some of those options disable saving or trophies. This site stays inside normal gameplay.

Rob clean: keep heat low on store, jewelry and bank jobs

Every robbery above pays more when you walk away without a long chase, because pocket cash and bags are lost if you are Busted or Wasted. Verified rules from Polygon, GameSpot and Destructoid (full breakdown in our GTA 6 wanted level and heat guide):

  1. Pick quiet targets. Police only come if a witness reports the crime or an alarm goes off. A shop with one clerk and no customers is ideal (Polygon, GameSpot).
  2. Mask on before, off after. Wear the mask during the job and remove it out of sight; putting one on after the crime looks suspicious (Polygon).
  3. Destroy the CCTV recorder, usually behind the counter in shops. If the camera icon shows a cloud, the footage cannot be deleted, so rely on the disguise (Polygon).
  4. Clear the heat icons once you break line of sight: swap hat and glasses, change outfit (keep a spare in the trunk), respray or swap the car, stash long guns, split Jason and Lucia up (Polygon, GameSpot).
  5. Leave the red heat zone before the next job; a new crime inside it brings your wanted level straight back (Destructoid).
  6. Bank first. Deposit at an ATM between jobs.

A simple late-game rotation

  1. Run a clean back-door jewelry or store job with the snap gun.
  2. Bank the cash immediately.
  3. While fences or shops are on cooldown, sell a car to Randy or a Pay ’N’ Spray, or run a derby at Ambrosia.
  4. When Pete’s contracts are up in Chapter 4, do a package run.
  5. At the end of Chapter 4, switch to bank vaults with Yasmani’s security-van tips.
  6. If you hear a robbery already in progress, consider taking the crew’s bags instead of starting a fresh job from scratch.

FAQ

When do you get the snap gun in GTA 6?

Launch guides place it in the Chapter 3 story. It is the tool that makes silent back-door robberies realistic.

How much do jewelry store robberies pay?

PowerPyx reports $10,000–$25,000 per jewelry store once Yasmani’s Jewelry Jobs unlock early in Chapter 3; PC Gamer puts clean back-door store jobs at $6,000–$15,000 and Polygon cites around $20,000 at good Vice City stores. Open the back-room safe and sell the jewelry to Yasmani.

How many cars are on Randy’s Wish List?

18 high-end sports cars, according to PowerPyx. The list unlocks halfway through Chapter 3 after Clonin’ Keys, Makin’ GS, and deliveries usually pay $30,000+ in perfect condition. The last car is the Truffade Thrax, the fastest car in GTA 6.

How do you rob banks in GTA 6?

Complete Credit Check in Chapter 3 to get the Dye-Pack Jammer (GamesRadar+), which lets you sell marked bills from small banks and Payday Loans. Large bank vaults (up to about $205,000) unlock at the end of Chapter 4 through the optional mission That’s Where The Money Is… (PowerPyx).

Who is Randy?

The later, high-end car contact in western Vice City. He pays more than Moses, cares about condition, gives you the Key Cloner and runs the Wish List of cars.

What is the best repeatable late-game money method?

There is no official answer. Launch-week guides point to jewelry stores and Randy’s cars in Chapter 3, Pilot Pete’s drops in Chapter 4, and large bank vaults from the end of Chapter 4 onward. Derby races are the most repeatable option that is not a crime spree. Compare every method in the money guide hub.

How do you rob stores in GTA 6 without getting a wanted level?

Choose a shop with no customers and a single clerk, wear a mask during the job, and destroy the CCTV recorder behind the counter before you leave. Police only respond if a witness reports the crime or an alarm goes off (Polygon, GameSpot; see our wanted level guide). If the CCTV icon shows a cloud, the footage cannot be deleted, so change your outfit and look and leave the heat zone.
