How Long Is GTA 6? Story Length, 100% Time and Hours by Playstyle

Most players will need 60 to 80 hours to finish GTA 6’s story. Reviewers reached the credits in anywhere from 42 hours (rushing) to 115 hours (doing everything along the way). Clearing the in-game 100% checklist and the Platinum trophy takes about 200 hours, and true completion of everything is estimated at 300+ hours. Below: times by playstyle, every published playthrough, missions per chapter, and how to plan your run.

Quick answer: how long is GTA 6?

  • Main story, rushed: about 42–53 hours. (Reported: Pure Xbox 42 h, PowerPyx ~50 h fast playstyle, Polygon ~53 h.)
  • Main story at a normal pace: 60–80 hours with some side content. (Reported: PowerPyx, Polygon, Eurogamer, IGN.)
  • Story plus lots of side activities: 90–115+ hours. (Reported: GameSpot 90–100 h, Polygon 100+ h, IGN 115 h.)
  • 100% Completion checklist / Platinum: about 200 hours. (Reported: PowerPyx; GameSpot estimate.)
  • True completion of everything: over 300 hours. (Reported: PowerPyx.)
  • Official Rockstar figure: none published.

How long does it take to 100% GTA 6?

PowerPyx puts the in-game 100% Completion checklist and Platinum trophy at about 200 hours, story included, and GameSpot estimates “upwards of 200 hours”. The good news, per PowerPyx:

  • Nothing on the 100% checklist is missable. You can free-roam after the story and finish everything.
  • The checklist asks for about 60% of “true” completion. For example, 100 of 255 animals and 200 of 277 vehicles, so you don’t need every single collectible.
  • You need 100 optional quests, so skipping side missions to rush the story only pushes the work to later.
  • The only gold medals are in challenges (42 shooting range and 13 race challenges), not in story missions.

Reviewers show how deep it goes: IGN’s Simon Cardy was at 71% after 70 hours, then spent another 30 hours exploring and gained only about 1%. See the full requirement list in our 100% checklist FAQ.

Can you keep playing GTA 6 after the story? (spoiler-light)

Light structure info only: no plot events, characters’ fates or ending details below.

Yes, but not in the usual GTA way. Rob Nelson, producer and co-studio head at Rockstar North, told The Verge (27 November 2026) that Jason and Lucia’s story “needed to end and have some kind of conclusion”, so the game doesn’t simply drop you back into the world after the credits. Instead:

  • You return to an earlier point. To keep playing, the game takes you back to a moment before a key late-game decision, and you can stay in that open free-roam period as long as you like (Nelson, The Verge).
  • It’s built for the long haul. Nelson describes it as having “the best single-player safehouse you’ve ever had in a GTA”, with a garage of supercars, a helipad and a gym.
  • It’s also how you see other endings. Nelson says the ending depends on how much money you have saved, what kind of criminal you were and key decisions at the end, with “a large number of potential outcomes”. Grinding more money in this period can change how things play out.

What that means for your planning: finishing the story doesn’t lock you out of the 100% checklist, and the money you save keeps mattering after the credits. Build it up with the Family Savings guide and the money guide, and keep your Criminal Profile in mind, since both feed into the ending. A manual save before the final chapter (see our save-slot plan) is still the quickest way to retry the final decisions.

Polygon calls GTA 6 “the series’ longest game by a wide margin”. A typical GTA 6 story run is roughly twice as long as GTA 5’s.

Why your run may take longer than you think

  • Money gates. Polygon notes several points where you must save a set amount of cash or wait for a call before the next story mission. Even story-only players end up doing side jobs. Our chapter guides cover the legit ways to earn: early game (Chapter 1), Port Gellhorn (Chapter 2) and Vice City (Chapters 3–4). Every target, from $5,000 in Family Savings to the $15,000 Vice City fund, is listed in the money guide hub.
  • Travel time is content. Leonida is huge, and Eurogamer’s Chris Tapsell points out how much of the game you discover while travelling. Skipping it with taxis saves hours but misses events.
  • Choices and relationship. IGN’s reviewers say Jason and Lucia’s relationship rating changes how the story and some missions play out, and they found different endings. Many players will want a second run.
  • No mission replay. IGN and PowerPyx report you can’t replay story missions, so trying another outcome means reloading a save or starting again.

How to plan your GTA 6 playthrough (step by step)

  1. Pick your pace. Budget about 50 hours if you mainline, 70–80 for a normal run, 100+ if you love side content. Reviewers recommend playing slowly; the game is built for it.
  2. Make a save right after the prologue. A second playthrough can skip the long intro. Manual saving unlocks only then; see how to save in GTA 6.
  3. Keep manual saves before big story moments. With no mission replay and more than one ending, an old save is the only way back.
  4. Take optional missions as they appear. You need 100 for the 100% checklist, and they help with story money gates.
  5. Scan cars and study animals from day one. 200 vehicle scans and 100 animals are on the checklist; doing them as you go saves dozens of hours of cleanup.
  6. Reveal the map early. PowerPyx unfogged the whole map in 2–3 hours with a crop duster. Jets are faster but have advanced trackers that bring a 4-star wanted level, and they burn fuel quickly.
  7. Bank your cash. Launch reports say you lose cash on hand when you die, so use ATMs and BuckMe before risky jobs.
  8. Leave challenges for last. Shooting range and race challenge golds are pure skill grinds. Track what’s left on our checklist.

How long is GTA 6? FAQ

How long is the GTA 6 story?

Most players should expect 60 to 80 hours. Published reviewer runs to the credits range from 42 hours (Pure Xbox, rushed) to 115 hours (IGN, stopping for everything). Eurogamer took about 65, IGN’s Simon Cardy 70, Polygon’s fastest run about 53, and PowerPyx 70 with optional quests. Rockstar has not published an official figure.

How long does it take to 100% GTA 6?

About 200 hours for the in-game 100% Completion checklist and the Platinum trophy, according to PowerPyx; GameSpot also estimates upwards of 200 hours. PowerPyx says true completion of everything in the game would take over 300 hours, because the checklist only asks for part of each category.

How many chapters are in GTA 6?

Five. Chapter 1 Just Like Paradise (22 main missions including the prologue), Chapter 2 Paradise Lost (11), Chapter 3 Tits, Ass, Drug Money (26), Chapter 4 Vice City, Baby (25) and Chapter 5 Top of the World, Ma (7), per GameSpot’s full mission list. There is no chapter select, so keep a manual save at the start and end of each chapter.

How many missions are in GTA 6?

GameSpot’s full list has 91 main missions across five chapters: 22 in Chapter 1 (including the prologue), 11 in Chapter 2, 26 in Chapter 3, 25 in Chapter 4 and 7 in Chapter 5. Our checklist tracks 94 story entries because some outlets also count optional missions.

Is GTA 6 longer than Red Dead Redemption 2?

Yes, for most players. Polygon cites HowLongToBeat data of about 52 hours for RDR2’s main story and 89 hours with side content, and expects 70 to 80 hours for GTA 6’s main story. HowLongToBeat lists about 32 hours for GTA 5’s story, so GTA 6 is the longest GTA by a wide margin.

Can you replay missions in GTA 6?

No. IGN and PowerPyx report there is no mission replay option and no mission gold medals. Keep manual saves before big story moments if you want to see a different outcome.

Does GTA 6 have multiple endings?

Yes. IGN’s Simon Cardy says he has found four endings so far and Luke Reilly two. Rockstar North’s Rob Nelson told The Verge there is “a large number of potential outcomes” based on how much money you saved, what kind of criminal you were and key final decisions. Rockstar hasn’t given an exact number, and we don’t spoil them here.

Can you play GTA 6 after beating the story?

Yes. After the credits, the game takes you back to a point before a key late decision, and you can free-roam there as long as you like with a fully kitted safehouse, then finish the story again for a different ending, according to Rob Nelson (The Verge, 27 November 2026). Nothing on the 100% checklist is missable (PowerPyx).
