GTA 6 early game money guide: how to make money fast in the Keys

Money is tight in the opening hours of Grand Theft Auto VI, and the best hustles are still locked. Here is how to earn honestly in Chapter 1 around the Leonida Keys — and what not to grind yet.

Updated 19 November 2026 (launch day). GTA 6 is out now on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S.

Updated as launch data drops — 19 November 2026. This is not an official Rockstar document. Payouts and unlock timing are launch-day reports from Polygon, PC Gamer, and GameSpot. Figures can vary. We do not cover cheats, glitches, or exploits.

Jason and Lucia are not rolling in cash yet, several story objectives later expect you to have savings, and the most profitable hustles are still locked. This guide covers the best legitimate, in-game ways to make money in Chapter 1 around the Leonida Keys and Key Lento.

How money works in GTA 6 (read this first)

Launch coverage describes three pots of money, not one:

  1. BuckMe (banked money). Safer. Deposit cash at ATMs when you can. Launch reports say banked funds remain if you go Wasted.
  2. Cash on hand. Useful for small buys, but Polygon’s first-hour guide says you lose cash you are carrying if you die. Early on, stash it (see below) or get it into an account.
  3. Loot in a bag. Stolen goods are not spendable cash until a fence buys them. If you go down carrying a bag, that take is gone.

Until banking is fully available, Polygon recommends putting early income under the loose planks in Jason’s bedroom. That stash survives if Jason dies. Do this before you go looking for trouble.

Two more early-game rules:

  • Do not blow your first earnings on clothes and guns. Polygon notes that early missions ask you to save money and even buy flowers. GameSpot also reports that some later story beats require cash before you can continue. Spend lightly in the Keys.
  • Push the story far enough to unlock the world. You cannot treat the first hour like GTA Online. Polygon says you generally cannot sell stolen cars, fast travel, or use most side activities until you are at least halfway through Chapter 1. Exploration is fun; grinding locked systems is not.

The best early money method: Kenton’s treasure dives

The most consistently recommended Chapter 1 hustle is diving for Kenton.

GameSpot reports that you meet Kenton early in the story. He runs a scuba-boat tourist shop in Key Lento and, on the side, still hunts wrecks. He is no longer the one getting wet; that is your job as Jason. After the relevant story beat (launch guides tie the activity to the mission Island Dreams), he can mark dive sites around the waters of the Keys.

How the loop works, based on launch-day write-ups:

  • Use a boat with dive gear. Kenton can provide a boat and scuba equipment at his dock; any suitable dive boat works.
  • Travel to a marked wreck (described as purple chest icons on the map in some reports).
  • Dive, sweep the area (a metal detector is mentioned in detailed launch guides), and bring treasure back up. You can surface for air and return later if a wreck is only half done.
  • Sell the haul to Kenton for cash, with no police chase attached to the job itself.

Reported payouts: one detailed launch guide puts the first wreck at about $1,400, the first two wrecks together around $2,400, and later sites potentially up to about $4,000 a dive. GameSpot says clearing every piece of treasure across Kenton’s sites can eventually add up to tens of thousands. Treat those as early estimates, not official ledgers.

Kenton’s work is slower than a mid-game car sale or a Chapter 3 jewelry job. In the Keys, that is fine: it is low heat, it is available early, and it does not spend the cash you need for the story.

What else actually pays in Chapter 1

Story missions

Follow the main path. Not every mission pays on the spot (Polygon is clear about that), but you come out ahead if you keep going, and you unlock the better hustles. This is the opposite of a “ignore the story and farm” game — at least at the start.

Small crimes, used carefully

You can mug NPCs or stick up a modest store if the game presents the opportunity. Polygon and PC Gamer both describe mugging as quick pocket change — typically a couple of hundred dollars at best — useful in a pinch, not a path to wealth. If you do rob someone, bank or stash the cash immediately. Getting Wasted with bills in your pocket wipes the gain.

Optional encounters

Keep an ear out for shouts, arrests, and odd NPC events while you move between missions. They are not a reliable income strategy in Chapter 1, but they teach the crime and police systems you will need later.

What not to grind yet

Skip these until the story opens them (usually Chapter 2 or 3):

  • Selling cars to Moses in Port Gellhorn, or to Randy later in Vice City
  • Pay ’N’ Spray car sales as a loop
  • Snap-gun back-door jewelry and store jobs
  • Serious bank work
  • Pilot Pete package drops
  • Bret’s hunting jobs (Jason only, reported from Chapter 2)

If a YouTube thumbnail promises “unlimited money in 10 minutes” during the prologue, it is either showing a later unlock, or it is the kind of cheat or exploit content we will not touch.

A practical first-session plan

  1. Play the opening missions and make a post-prologue save, as Polygon suggests, so you are not stuck replaying the intro.
  2. After your first payday, stash cash in Jason’s bedroom and deposit at an ATM whenever BuckMe is available.
  3. Continue the story until Kenton’s dives unlock, then clear a wreck or two between missions.
  4. Spend only what the story requires.
  5. When Chapter 2 moves you toward Port Gellhorn, switch to our mid-game money guide — that is when Moses, hunting, and steadier hustles come online.

FAQ

What is the fastest way to make money in GTA 6 right at the start?

There is no huge, repeatable early exploit, and we would not publish one if there were. The best honest loop in Chapter 1 is progressing the story and running Kenton’s treasure dives once they unlock.

Can I sell cars in Chapter 1?

Launch reports say regular car-selling (Moses, then later Randy and Pay ’N’ Spray loops) opens as the story moves on, not in the first hour. Do not spend the opening session looking for a fence that is not there yet.

Should I spend money on weapons and clothes early?

Keep it light. Several launch guides warn that the story expects you to have cash on hand for later objectives.

Is this official information from Rockstar?

The release platforms and setting are official. The payouts and activity unlocks are launch-day press reports, labeled as such, and will be updated.
