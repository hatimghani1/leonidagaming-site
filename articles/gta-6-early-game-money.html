GTA 6 early game money guide: how to make money fast in the Keys

Money is tight in the opening hours of Grand Theft Auto VI, the best hustles are still locked, and almost everything you earn is wiped when Chapter 1 ends. Here is how to earn honestly around the Leonida Keys, what to do with your cash before Tourist Trap, and what not to grind yet.

Jason and Lucia are not rolling in cash yet, several story objectives later expect you to have savings, and the most profitable hustles are still locked. This guide covers the best legitimate, in-game ways to make money in Chapter 1 around the Leonida Keys and Key Lento.

How money works in GTA 6 (read this first)

Launch coverage describes three pots of money, not one:

  1. BuckMe (banked money). Safer. Deposit cash at ATMs when you can. Your BuckMe app has a current account and a Family Savings account (see below). Game Rant says an arrest takes a bail fee from the current account; Family Savings is never touched.
  2. Cash on hand. Useful for small buys, but Polygon’s first-hour guide says you lose cash you are carrying if you die. Early on, stash it (see below) or get it into an account.
  3. Loot in a bag. Stolen goods are not spendable cash until a fence buys them. If you go down carrying a bag, that take is gone.

Until banking is fully available, Polygon recommends putting early income under the loose planks in Jason’s bedroom. That stash survives if Jason dies, but not the end of the chapter: Game Rant reports the house stash is lost after Tourist Trap, so empty it before then.

Two more early-game rules:

  • Do not blow your first earnings straight away. Polygon notes that early missions ask you to save money and even buy flowers. Keep enough for those story buys, then follow the reset plan below: anything left in your wallet or current account before Tourist Trap is better spent on guns or clothes, which carry over.
  • Push the story far enough to unlock the world. You cannot treat the first hour like GTA Online. Polygon says you generally cannot sell stolen cars, fast travel, or use most side activities until you are at least halfway through Chapter 1. Exploration is fun; grinding locked systems is not.

The Chapter 1 money reset: what you lose and what you keep

Light spoiler (no story details). PowerPyx calls farming money in Chapter 1 “pointless” because you lose it at the end of the chapter. Game Rant (20 and 21 November) explains what that means:

At the end of Chapter 1What happens (Game Rant)
Pocket cashLost
BuckMe current accountReset to zero
Stash in Jason’s houseLost, along with access to the house
Jason’s carLost (so don’t pay for upgrades)
BuckMe Family SavingsKept. Chapter 1 limit: $5,000. Can never be withdrawn.
Guns you ownKept: take them back from the weapons locker at Ammu-Nation in Chapter 2
Clothes you boughtKept in your wardrobe

The point of no return is the mission Tourist Trap: Game Rant says it locks you into three back-to-back missions, and you do not get free roam again until Chapter 2. It only starts when you pick Lucia up from work after Kat and Mattia call about the pickup job, so you can delay it as long as you like; KeenGamer notes that the mission Recalibration (with Cal) leads straight into that call, so treat Recalibration as your last warning. The same warning was one of the top r/GTA6 posts of launch week (reported by ComicBook on 21 November). Before you start it:

  1. Make a manual save you keep, so you can come back to anything you missed (how to save in GTA 6).
  2. Empty the stash in Jason’s house and deposit it at an ATM.
  3. Collect and sell Donnie’s lost drugs (swamp drops southwest of Hamlet, plus a boat east of Corsair Key after Swamped, per GamesRadar+). KeenGamer adds that Donnie stops buying after the mission Swamped, so sell before it.
  4. Open the BuckMe app and move $5,000 into Family Savings (“Buck Me”, choose the amount, confirm). This also completes the first Save for the Future goal.
  5. Spend what is left on guns, attachments or clothes. Do not spend it on Jason’s car.
  6. Finish the non-money missables too (Shirley’s favours, the 5 books in Jason’s house, meditating with Lucia): full list in our before Tourist Trap checklist.
  7. Wrap up the Chapter 1 side activities you care about. GamesRadar+ says Kenton’s shipwrecks and Cal’s fishing come back later, so you will not lose the dives for good.

Chapter 2 starts nearly broke, so your first job there is selling cars to Moses. The full picture for every chapter is in the money guide hub.

The best early money method: Kenton’s treasure dives

The most consistently recommended Chapter 1 hustle is diving for Kenton.

GameSpot reports that you meet Kenton early in the story. He runs a scuba-boat tourist shop in Key Lento and, on the side, still hunts wrecks. He is no longer the one getting wet; that is your job as Jason. After the relevant story beat (launch guides tie the activity to the mission Island Dreams), he can mark dive sites around the waters of the Keys.

How the loop works, based on launch-day write-ups:

  • Use a boat with dive gear. Kenton can provide a boat and scuba equipment at his dock; any suitable dive boat works.
  • Travel to a marked wreck (described as purple chest icons on the map in some reports).
  • Dive, sweep the area (a metal detector is mentioned in detailed launch guides), and bring treasure back up. You can surface for air and return later if a wreck is only half done.
  • Sell the haul to Kenton for cash, with no police chase attached to the job itself.

Reported payouts: one detailed launch guide puts the first wreck at about $1,400, the first two wrecks together around $2,400, and later sites potentially up to about $4,000 a dive. GameSpot says clearing every piece of treasure across Kenton’s sites can eventually add up to tens of thousands. Treat those as early estimates, not official ledgers.

Kenton’s work is slower than a mid-game car sale or a Chapter 3 jewelry job. In the Keys, that is fine: it is low heat and available early. Because the cash is wiped at the end of the chapter, treat dives as a way to fill your $5,000 Family Savings and buy gear, not as a long grind. PC Gamer calls diving for treasure “mildly lucrative” and “extremely time consuming.”

What else actually pays in Chapter 1

Story missions

Follow the main path. Not every mission pays on the spot (Polygon is clear about that), but you come out ahead if you keep going, and you unlock the better hustles. This is the opposite of a “ignore the story and farm” game — at least at the start.

Small crimes, used carefully

You can mug NPCs or stick up a modest store if the game presents the opportunity. Polygon and PC Gamer both describe mugging as quick pocket change — typically a couple of hundred dollars at best — useful in a pinch, not a path to wealth. If you do rob someone, bank or stash the cash immediately. Getting Wasted with bills in your pocket wipes the gain.

Gang operations and races

PowerPyx’s Chapter 1 list is short: play the story, rob stores (aim at the clerk and take the register), rob gang operations (yellow map icons) and win races. These are enough to reach the $5,000 Family Savings goal and pay for a few guns. Gang operations are real gunfights, so go in equipped.

Optional encounters

Keep an ear out for shouts, arrests, and odd NPC events while you move between missions. They are not a reliable income strategy in Chapter 1, but they teach the crime and police systems you will need later.

What not to grind yet

Skip these until the story opens them (usually Chapter 2 or 3):

  • Selling cars to Moses in Port Gellhorn, or to Randy later in Vice City
  • Pay ’N’ Spray car sales as a loop
  • Snap-gun back-door jewelry and store jobs
  • Serious bank work
  • Pilot Pete package drops
  • Bret’s hunting jobs (Jason only, reported from Chapter 2)

If a YouTube thumbnail promises “unlimited money in 10 minutes” during the prologue, it is either showing a later unlock, or it is the kind of cheat or exploit content we will not touch.

A practical first-session plan

  1. Play the opening missions and make a post-prologue save, as Polygon suggests, so you are not stuck replaying the intro.
  2. After your first payday, stash cash in Jason’s bedroom and deposit at an ATM whenever BuckMe is available.
  3. Continue the story until Kenton’s dives unlock, then clear a wreck or two between missions.
  4. Once you have the BuckMe app, move $5,000 into Family Savings.
  5. Before Tourist Trap, empty the house stash and spend leftover cash on guns or clothes (see the reset plan).
  6. When Chapter 2 moves you toward Port Gellhorn, switch to our mid-game money guide — that is when Moses, hunting, and steadier hustles come online.

FAQ

What is the fastest way to make money in GTA 6 right at the start?

There is no huge, repeatable early exploit, and we would not publish one if there were. In Chapter 1 the best plan is to progress the story, rob a few stores or gang operations, win races and run Kenton’s treasure dives once they unlock, then put $5,000 into Family Savings before Tourist Trap.

Do you lose your money at the end of Chapter 1 in GTA 6?

Yes. Game Rant reports that you lose your pocket cash, your BuckMe current account balance and the stash in Jason’s house when Chapter 1 ends, after the point-of-no-return mission Tourist Trap. Money in BuckMe Family Savings (up to $5,000 in Chapter 1), guns and clothes carry over. See the reset plan.

When does Tourist Trap start in GTA 6, and can I delay it?

Tourist Trap starts when you pick Lucia up from work after Kat and Mattia call to say the pickup job is back on (Game Rant). Nothing forces you to go, so you can keep playing Chapter 1, bank $5,000 in Family Savings and finish missables first. KeenGamer says the mission Recalibration leads straight into that call. Full checklist: what to do before Tourist Trap.

Should I put money in Family Savings in GTA 6?

Yes, up to $5,000 before Tourist Trap. Game Rant says Family Savings money can never be withdrawn, but it can never be lost either, so it is the only way to carry Chapter 1 money into Chapter 2. Later, only save what you do not need for story targets such as the $15,000 Prepare for Vice City goal.

Can I sell cars in Chapter 1?

Not to a fence. Launch guides say selling stolen cars opens with Moses in Chapter 2, then Pay ’n’ Spray (Standard Tracker cars) and Randy in Chapter 3.

Should I spend money on weapons and clothes early?

Keep enough for early story purchases, but spend what is left before Tourist Trap. Game Rant reports that guns (recoverable from the Ammu-Nation weapons locker in Chapter 2) and clothes carry over, while cash and your current account do not.

Is this official information from Rockstar?

The release platforms and setting are official. Payouts, unlocks and the Chapter 1 reset come from launch-week guides (Game Rant, PowerPyx, GamesRadar+, Polygon, PC Gamer, GameSpot), labeled as such, and will be updated.
